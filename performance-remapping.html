Honest advice on whether a remap is right for your car

A remap changes how an engine is configured to run: how it manages boost, fuelling, ignition timing and a long list of calibration values held in the ECU. Done properly, it's a tailored setup matched to one specific car. Done carelessly, it's a generic file written for a different engine, in a different state of health, wearing different parts — loaded onto yours.

What tailored mapping means here

  • The vehicle is assessed before any map is written
  • The map is tailored to the car — its condition, its use, and what you want from it
  • The car is checked before and after the remap
  • Honest advice if a remap isn't the right move for your car

Diagnostics first — it's the same discipline

The reason remapping sits naturally alongside our diagnostics work is that both start the same way: establish the true condition of the vehicle before changing anything. A performance car with an underlying running fault, a tired sensor or an unresolved warning light is not a candidate for a new map — it's a candidate for diagnosis. That's why the assessment comes before the map, and why we'll sometimes tell a customer their car needs sorting before remapping is even on the table.

What a tailored map is for

Drivers come to us for a remap because they want the car set up properly for how they use it — not a one-size-fits-all file with numbers attached that may or may not suit their engine. The honest version of that conversation includes the option of not remapping at all. If we don't think a map is right for your car, we'll say so — honest advice is the thing customers say they value most.

Before and after

Every remap is bracketed by checks — the car is assessed before any map is written, and checked again after. You get a straightforward explanation of what was done, and what to expect from the car now it's done.
